# IBM DisplayWriter Beam Spring Keyboard Restoration (2026)

![The restored IBM DisplayWriter keyboard](images/post_restoration.jpg)

This is a detailed account of restoring a vintage IBM DisplayWriter beam spring keyboard, from initial disassembly and repair of damaged switches through cleaning, reassembly, installation of a modern controller, and firmware configuration.

I've documented what worked, what didn't, and the custom tools and fixtures I made along the way. My hope is that this will be both an interesting restoration story and a useful reference for anyone working on vintage beam spring keyboards.

## Contents

1. [Introduction](01-introduction.md) — Why I took on the project, the keyboard's initial condition, restoration goals, and precautions.
2. [Disassembly](02-disassembly.md) — Opening the case, removing keycaps and the switch assembly, and handling delicate mechanisms.
3. [Restoring the Switches](03-switch-restoration.md) — Disassembly, cleaning, repairs, custom fixtures, reassembly, testing, and grading.
4. [Cleaning the Components](04-cleaning.md) — Cleaning the case, plate, pad card, keycaps, and other components.
5. [Restoring Other Parts](05-other-restoration.md) — Straightening the base pan, replacing foam, and repairing keycaps and case damage.
6. [Reassembly](06-reassembly.md) — Putting the restored switch assembly and keyboard back together.
7. [Controller Assembly](07-controller.md) — Choosing, building, and installing a modern controller, including the card-edge connection and speaker.
8. [Firmware and Key Map](08-firmware.md) — Building and flashing firmware, diagnosing the matrix, and configuring the layout.
9. [The Finished Keyboard](09-finished.md) — The outcome, typing impressions, and possible future work.

## About this report

This documents the methods I used on *my particular keyboard*, not a definitive service procedure. Vintage parts can be fragile, and some of the techniques described involve risks or tradeoffs. Read the relevant section in full before attempting a procedure, and use your own judgment about what is appropriate for your hardware.
